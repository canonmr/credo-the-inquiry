# AUT-006 -- Infallibility: The Claim and Its History

## The claim in plain words

Infallibility is a very specific claim: the Pope cannot err only when he teaches ex cathedra — as pastor and teacher of all the faithful, definitively, on faith or morals — and that definition is "irreformable of themselves, and not by reason of the consent of the Church" (Pastor Aeternus 4; compare LG 25; CCC 891).

Now history: in 681, the Third Council of Constantinople — recognized by Catholics as ecumenical — condemned Pope Honorius I as a heretic. Defenders answer: Honorius did not teach ex cathedra. So the question becomes: are the conditions applied honestly, or designed to make the claim immune? That debate is unfinished.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove infallibility false, and does not prove Honorius a heretic. It affirms: the infallibility claim is tightly defined — and because it is tight, it can be tested. That test (whether the conditions are applied consistently) is unfinished, and must not be treated as finished.
