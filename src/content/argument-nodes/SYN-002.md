# SYN-002 -- What the Inquiry Established

## The claim in plain words

The inquiry was not wasted: across six chapters it produced a definite body of results. The church's claims are exact and quotable — the address, the books, the interpreter, the founder, the believer's end. The historical picture is thin but real: a teacher executed under Pilate; an early, public, creedal confession of resurrection (1 Cor 15:3-8; Tacitus; Josephus). The definitions are precise: Nicea 325, the creeds, the Catechism.

And the tests are on record: the fruit-test (James 2:17; Gal 5:22-23), the church's own admission of the stakes (1 Cor 15:19).

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not claim the documented items prove the church's claims true. Documentation is not proof. What the list establishes is that the dispute is about decisions, not facts — and that is the finding.
