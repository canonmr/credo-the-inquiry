# POE-003 — The Parent Analogy

## Plain-language statement

If a parent sees a child about to suffer and could prevent it cheaply, we blame the parent for not preventing it. God sees, can prevent, and has not. The analogy is intuitive and the moral pressure is real.

The chapter will not let the analogy carry more weight than it can bear. Three points of difference are usually raised: divine knowledge is infallible, divine purposes are not local, and divine resources are not finite. The chapter holds the analogy lightly and asks where the joints break.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The parent analogy does not establish that God is guilty of anything. It establishes only that the question 'why this suffering?' is morally serious, and that the question cannot be dismissed by appealing to foreknowledge alone.
