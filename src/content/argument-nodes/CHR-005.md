# CHR-005 -- The Sacraments: What the Church Claims They Do

## The claim in plain words

The church does not claim that baptism and Eucharist are symbols — it claims they do what only God can do. "Go... baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matt 28:19) — and baptism, says the Catechism (1213), frees from sin and gives new birth. "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you" (John 6:53) — and the Eucharist, says the Catechism (1324), is "the source and summit of the Christian life", containing "Christ himself".

The claims are precise. And they are unverifiable: grace cannot be measured, new birth cannot be observed. What can be shown is the claim itself — and that is what this node does.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not establish that baptism frees from sin or that the Eucharist is the body of Christ. It establishes that the church makes these claims, with these texts, in these words — and that their content is a promise, not a provable effect. Whether the promise is kept is a matter of trust — and trust was the subject of chapter 4.
