# SCR-006 -- The Books' Purpose: Witness to Faith

## The claim in plain words

Before assessing a document, ask: what was it written for? John answers for his own book: "these are written so that you may come to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God" (John 20:30-31). Luke writes so that Theophilus may be certain (Luke 1:4).

So the books are witness to faith, not police reports. That is not an excuse to ignore historical questions — but it changes the criterion of assessment: what is tested is the honesty of the testimony, not the completeness of the facts. A love letter is not wrong because it omits dates; and a witness to faith is not false because it does not record everything.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The new criterion does not prove the testimony honest. Suspicion of interested witnesses remains legitimate — and must still be tested case by case. What is rejected is only one fallacy: measuring a book of witness with the yardstick of a science textbook.
