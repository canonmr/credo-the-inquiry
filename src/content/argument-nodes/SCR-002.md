# SCR-002 -- The Canon Was Formed in History

## The claim in plain words

When you buy a Bible, it looks like one book: cover, binding, table of contents. But the table of contents is itself a piece of history. Catholics, Protestants, and Orthodox do not share the same list. And for the New Testament, the first complete list we possess appears only in 367 AD — in the Easter letter of Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria, who called the books "fountains of salvation".

That means: a community chose, and that choice can be traced, debated, and questioned.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not judge either the Catholic or the Protestant canon. It only moves the question: not "are these 27 books canonical?" but "who chose them, how, and why should we trust that choice?" That question cannot be answered by opening the Bible — because the Bible you open is the result of that decision.
