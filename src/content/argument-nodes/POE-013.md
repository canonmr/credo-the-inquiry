# POE-013 — Skeptical Theism

## Plain-language statement

The evidential problem of evil depends on the no-seeum inference: from 'we cannot see a reason for this suffering' to 'there is no reason for this suffering'. Skeptical theism challenges that inference. If God exists, God is infinite; we are finite. There is no reason to expect that a finite mind can see all the reasons of an infinite mind. The absence of seen reasons is therefore not evidence of the absence of reasons.

The response is powerful, but it is not free. Critics have argued that skeptical theism is self-defeating: if we cannot see God's reasons for suffering, we cannot see God's reasons for the goods either, and theism loses its evidential support. The chapter holds the tension: skeptical theism is the most important contemporary response to the evidential problem, and the self-defeat objection is the most important objection to it.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that God exists. It does not establish that the asymmetry between the suffering-domain and other domains can be maintained. It only establishes that the no-seeum inference — on which the evidential problem depends — is not as secure as it first appears.
