# POE-007 — The Logical Problem of Evil

## Plain-language statement

If God is all-powerful and all-good, why is there evil? In its strongest form, the question asks whether the three propositions (God is omnipotent, God is wholly good, evil exists) can even be consistent. J. L. Mackie argued in 1955 that, with two added premises (a good thing eliminates evil as far as it can; an omnipotent thing has no limits), the three propositions are inconsistent. The conclusion was atheism.

Plantinga's 1977 reply showed that the inconsistency depends on the two added premises, and that both are deniable. Once they are denied, the logical problem of evil is dissolved. The interesting question moves elsewhere: it is no longer 'can God and evil coexist logically?' but 'is the actual world's distribution of evil the one we should expect from a good God?'

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that God and evil are intuitively incompatible. It does not establish the evidential case against theism. It only attempts a *formal* contradiction, and the contemporary consensus is that the formal contradiction fails.
