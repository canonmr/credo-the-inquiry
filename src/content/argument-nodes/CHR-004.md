# CHR-004 -- The Divinity of Christ: Nicaea's Answer

## The claim in plain words

In 325, the first ecumenical council gave the church its answer on the divinity of Christ: "true God from true God, begotten not made, of one substance with the Father." The Greek word homoousios — "of one substance" — was chosen deliberately to exclude the position of Arius, who taught that Christ was a creature, the greatest of creatures but not God.

The definition is precise. And its content is not verifiable: no method can check whether Christ is "of one substance with the Father". Nicaea's answer is a definition of faith — made in history, about what is beyond history.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove that Christ is truly God, and does not prove Nicaea right. It shows: the church answered its own question with a precise word, in a real controversy with real politics. The definition is examined here as a historical claim with a theological content — and that content remains a confession, not a verified fact.
