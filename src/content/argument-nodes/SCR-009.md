# SCR-009 -- The Unproven Claim of Inspiration

## The claim in plain words

This is the strongest objection to the whole chapter, and it must be spoken plainly: no single step proves that the books are inspired. History can show that Luke wrote carefully — not that God spoke through him. The canon can show how the church chose — not that its choice was right. The dual-author model explains the claim — but it works just as well for the scriptures of other religions.

And there is a fact we cannot set aside: other books also claim inspiration. A claim is not unique; uniqueness, if any, must be shown. The criterion of chapter 2 demands this — and that criterion must not be loosened in chapter 3 merely because the text is dear.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove the books false, and does not prove inspiration impossible. It proves something smaller but more disturbing: the claim of inspiration is still a claim — not yet a demonstrated fact. That burden is carried into the next chapter.
