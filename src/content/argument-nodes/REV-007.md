# REV-007 -- Faith as Response

## The claim in plain words

There are two ways of receiving a truth. The first: forced by evidence. If you see a cloudy sky and a falling barometer, you conclude it will rain. There is no decision there — evidence at work.

The second: responded to. A friend extends a hand and says: "Let's be friends." No evidence forces you to take the hand. You may refuse; you may accept. There is no conclusion there — there is a response.

Catechism 143 says faith is the surrender of intellect and will to God. That is the language of response, not of conclusion. And this is where hiddenness finds its place: God does not shine his face with a coercive light, because coercive light does not produce love — it produces compliance. God addresses with a voice that can be ignored, because only a voice that can be ignored can be freely responded to.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node rests on one big "if": if the address is real. Faith as response explains how hiddenness and love can coexist — but it does not prove that God truly addresses. Someone who hears no address cannot be told to "respond". The question of whether the address is real remains open — and it demands evidence, not feeling.
