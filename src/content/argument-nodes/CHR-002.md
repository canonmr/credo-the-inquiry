# CHR-002 -- The Historical Jesus: What the Records Allow

## The claim in plain words

What can history say about Jesus? A thin but real portrait: a Galilean teacher named Jesus was executed under Pontius Pilate around 30 AD, and his followers proclaimed him risen. Tacitus, writing c. 116 as a hostile Roman, attests the execution; Josephus, c. 93, attests the teacher and the cross; the church's own documents (1 Cor 15, Acts 2) attest the proclamation. "We declare to you what we have seen and heard" (1 John 1:3).

The execution is as well attested as any event of antiquity. The confession — that this man is the Christ — is not attested by history. History reaches the foot of the cross and stops.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node establishes the historical floor: a man, a teacher, a crucifixion. It does not establish that Jesus was the Christ, and does not establish the resurrection. The floor is real — and the confession is beyond it.
