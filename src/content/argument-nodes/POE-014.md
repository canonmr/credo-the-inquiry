# POE-014 — Could God Have Created a Better World?

## Plain-language statement

CCC §310 explicitly says: 'With infinite power God could always create something better.' This is not a denial that the actual world is good; it is an admission that the question 'could God have done better?' is real and open. The Catholic tradition does not require the claim that this is the best of all possible worlds. It requires only that God has a reason for creating this world rather than a better one, and that the reason may be one we cannot fully see in this life (CCC §314).

The theist is then committed to specifying the reason. The Catholic tradition offers a cluster of candidates: free will, soul-making, the regularity of the natural order, the eschatological defeat of evil. None is fully worked out. The work is real and unfinished.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that this is the best of all possible worlds. It does not establish that the reason for creating this world rather than a better one is known to us. It only establishes that the comparative question is real and open, and that the Catholic tradition explicitly allows it.
