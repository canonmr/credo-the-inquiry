# REV-003 -- Can God Speak?

## The claim in plain words

Imagine a brilliant author sending a novel to a reader. The author has never met the reader, has no body in the same room — but the novel speaks. The words he wrote carry meaning: hopes, fears, plans. The reader understands the author without ever seeing his face.

The objection that God cannot speak because he has no body works like this: "Language is a tool of embodied creatures; God has no body; therefore God is mute." But our own experience refutes its first premise. We talk about justice without a body of justice in the room. We understand someone's intent through a letter, through a novel, through a work of art. Meaning does not demand the body of its carrier.

If so, there is no logical obstacle to God conveying truth — through promptings that move the heart, through events that speak, through inspired human beings writing words. Whether that actually happens is another matter. But the logical door is open.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node only establishes that God *can* speak. It does not show that God actually speaks, and it gives us no tool for distinguishing a divine address from the voice of our own heart. That is the work of the following nodes — and far more difficult work.
