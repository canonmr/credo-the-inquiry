# SCR-003 -- Inspiration Means Two Authors

## The claim in plain words

What does it mean for a book to be "inspired by God"? The naive answer: God spelled out the words and the writer merely copied. The Catholic answer is subtler: God and the human writer are both truly authors — like two voices in one text. Paul writes "all scripture is inspired by God" (theopneustos: God-breathed). Dei Verbum 11 teaches that God "chose men... so that they, using their own abilities and powers, wrote as true authors". Catechism 110 adds the incarnation analogy: as the Word became flesh, the word of God became human words.

This model keeps both sides: the Bible is fully God's word, and fully human words — with all the marks of its age.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The dual-author model explains what tradition claims — it does not prove the claim true. The question remains open: if the writers are truly human, how far did God "write" in them? The next node pursues that question.
