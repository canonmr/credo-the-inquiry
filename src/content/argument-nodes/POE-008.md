# POE-008 — Plantinga's Free Will Defense

## Plain-language statement

Plantinga argued in 1977 that the logical problem of evil rests on two premises that can be denied: (a) a good thing always eliminates evil as far as it can, and (b) an omnipotent thing has no limits to what it can do. The denial of (a) is the substantive move: a good thing might have reason to allow evil for the sake of a greater good, including the existence of free creatures who can love and choose well. The denial of (b) is the modal move: even an omnipotent being cannot actualize a world that is logically impossible, including (on Plantinga's hypothesis) a world of free creatures who always freely do what is right.

The result is a *defense* — an argument that God and evil are not logically incompatible. It is not a theodicy; it does not say that the actual world's distribution of evil is the best one. The remaining work — the evidential problem, the question of actual reasons — is left to other nodes.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that the actual world's distribution of evil is justified. It does not establish that God has morally sufficient reasons for the specific evils that occur. It only establishes that the *existence* of God and the *existence* of evil are not, by themselves, a logical contradiction.
