# CHR-009 -- The Strongest Objection: A Claim Chosen, Not Proven

## The claim in plain words

The strongest objection to the church's claims about Christ must be spoken without comfort: they are not proven — they are chosen. History gives a crucified teacher; faith confesses the risen Son of God; both readings fit the same evidence. "It is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone, to believe anything upon insufficient evidence" (Clifford, 1877).

The confession exceeds the evidence. That is not an insult to the claim — it is the honest description of its status. And the believer's answer is equally honest: "For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son" (John 3:16) — the exceeding is the point of the claim, not its flaw.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove the church's claims false, and does not prove faith irrational. It establishes the boundary: the confession is a choice in the presence of evidence — and the debate between Clifford and the believer (should belief ever exceed evidence?) is a living philosophical question. The objection and the confession stand face to face.
