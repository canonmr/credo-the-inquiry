# AUT-004 -- The Circle of Interpretation

## The claim in plain words

Chapter 3 found a circle: the church chose the books that contain claims about the church. Chapter 4 finds the same circle in a sharper form: the church that chose the books also claims the right to interpret them (Dei Verbum 10; Catechism 85).

If the church interprets, its reading cannot be tested against the text without borrowing the church's interpretation. If every reader interprets for himself, that reading cannot be tested without borrowing the reader's authority. There is no neutral referee's chair — the chair is empty.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The circle does not prove the church wrong — and does not prove the individual reader right. It affirms a structure: the question of interpretive authority is not settled by argument, but by choice. Two exits are tried in the next nodes: the Protestant exit (chapter 4, section 6) and the infallibility exit (chapter 4, section 7).
