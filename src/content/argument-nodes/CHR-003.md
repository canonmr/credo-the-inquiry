# CHR-003 -- The Resurrection: The Founding Claim

## The claim in plain words

The church was not founded on a book — it was founded on a proclamation: "God raised him up, having freed him from death" (Acts 2:24). And the proclamation is carried by an early creed: "that Christ died for our sins... that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day... and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve... then to more than five hundred brothers and sisters at one time... then to James, then to all the apostles" (1 Cor 15:3-7).

Paul quotes a creed he "received" — a tradition fixed before his letters, within a few years of the crucifixion. The claim is early, public, and central. Whether it is true is the debate of this chapter — and of every chapter after it.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The early creed establishes that the resurrection claim is old, public, and foundational. It does not establish that the resurrection happened. Earliness is not truth; it is urgency. The claim's content remains exactly what is contested.
