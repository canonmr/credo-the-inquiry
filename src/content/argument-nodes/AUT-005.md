# AUT-005 -- The Protestant Challenge: Scripture Alone

## The claim in plain words

There is one sharp answer to the magisterium's claim: no. In 1520 Luther rejected the division of 'clergy and laity' as "lying and hypocrisy": all Christians are priests, and Scripture is open to all. No office has the right to fix a binding meaning.

But there is a price to pay: "no prophecy of scripture is a matter of one's own interpretation" (2 Pet 1:20). If private interpretation is forbidden and the magisterium is rejected — who interprets? Sola scriptura does not break the circle; it moves it from the office to the reader.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove sola scriptura wrong or the magisterium right. It shows the cost of both positions: the magisterium demands trust in an office; sola scriptura demands trust in a reading. Neither can be avoided — only chosen.
