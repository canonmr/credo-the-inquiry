# POE-005 — Permission Is Not Intention

## Plain-language statement

When a parent allows a child to fail, hoping the child will learn, the parent does not intend the failure. The parent intends the learning and permits the failure as a means or a cost. Permission and intention are distinct.

Applied to the divine case: even if God permits evil, that does not by itself mean God intends evil. The chapter holds this distinction firmly and then asks the next question: what reason could possibly justify the permission? That question is the heart of the problem.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that God's permission of evil is justified. It only establishes the conceptual distinction. The justification question — what reason could possibly justify permitting this — is left fully open.
