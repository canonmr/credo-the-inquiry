# POE-011 — Natural Evil

## Plain-language statement

Earthquakes, diseases, congenital disorders: these do not arise from anyone's free choice. They are the by-product of a regular natural order. The theodicies for natural evil must therefore do different work from the theodicies for moral evil. The candidate accounts are: (a) the value of a regular order (Swinburne), (b) soul-making (Hick), (c) the participation of non-human free agents (Plantinga). Each has known costs. The Catholic tradition, in CCC §310, holds that physical evil exists alongside physical good 'as long as creation has not reached perfection', but does not identify the cause of every particular natural evil.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that any specific natural evil has a specific cause. It does not establish that fallen angels are the cause of any particular natural disaster. It only establishes that natural evil is theologically distinct from moral evil, and that theodicy for the two is different work.
