# POE-004 — Why the Divine Case Is Different

## Plain-language statement

The parent analogy has force because we expect parents to act locally, with limited knowledge, on what they can see. God is not in that situation. Divine knowledge is not fallible, divine purposes are not local, divine resources are not limited. These three differences mean that the moral pressure the analogy exerts on the parent does not transfer automatically to God.

This is the conservative move. It does not yet say what God's reasons are. It only says that the question of God's reasons must be asked on its own terms.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The argument does not establish that God has reasons. It establishes only that the parent analogy, by itself, does not settle the moral question. Reasons — if they exist — must be specified in their own terms.
