# REV-002 -- Two Kinds of Revelation

## The claim in plain words

There are two doors often mistaken for one. The first door: nature. When you look at the sky, the sea, and the stars, something makes some people murmur: "Something must have made all this." That is natural revelation — the soft voice that comes from creation itself, audible to anyone willing to think.

The second door: history. When a prophet says "Thus says the LORD", or when a disciple writes about the words of Jesus, there is a stronger claim: not just "something made the world", but "God has spoken to us". That is special revelation.

Mixing the two muddies the debate. If you demand that nature prove God's plan of salvation, you are asking of the first door what only the second can give. If you demand that Scripture be provable like a law of nature, you are asking of the second door what only the first can give. Each door has its own key.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove that the second door is really open — that special revelation actually occurs. It only puts the right labels on both doors. Whether there is a voice behind the second door is the question of the rest of this chapter.
