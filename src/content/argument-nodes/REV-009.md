# REV-009 -- Criteria for an Address

## The claim in plain words

Imagine two people who claim to hear a voice in the middle of the night. One says: "It was my mother calling me." The other says: "It was a neighbor asking for help." Without criteria — without looking, without examining, without checking — both claims are equally possible and equally empty.

So too with the claim "God speaks". It is a serious claim — but it stands next to other serious claims: "It was only my own thought." "It was only my family's tradition." "It was only a wish that wanted to believe." Without criteria, we could never tell which is true. The principle of difference: a claim that cannot be distinguished from its alternatives carries no information.

But — and this is important — the criteria exist. Historical testimony: did the claimed events really happen? Coherence: is the text consistent with itself? Fruit: does the claim change lives for the better? Community: has the testimony been handed down honestly? These criteria do not give mathematical certainty — but they give an honest map. And an honest map is what we promised in this inquiry.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove that special revelation actually occurs, or that Scripture passes all the criteria. It sets the rules of the game: revelation claims must be assessed with examinable criteria — not accepted because comfortable, not rejected because frightening. The verdict itself awaits the next chapter.
