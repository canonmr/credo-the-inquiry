# REV-004 -- Schellenberg's Argument from Hiddenness

## The claim in plain words

J. L. Schellenberg asks a very human question. Imagine someone — a serious teenager, a mother who has just lost her child, a professor willing to give up everything for the truth — who prays, seeks, reads, and waits with all their heart. And then nothing happens. The sky stays silent. No answer, no sign, no presence.

Schellenberg says: if God were perfect love, God would surely want a relationship with such a person. And a God who wants a relationship would make sure the person could find him. But this person finds nothing. So if God exists, he is not perfectly loving — or he does not exist at all.

This argument is strong because it attacks God not with theoretical logic but with the testimony of honest people. It is also fair: it does not accuse them of "fighting God". It names their situation accurately — nonresistant nonbelief — and says: this is what should not happen if a perfectly loving God exists.

## What this argument does NOT establish

The argument rests on two attackable points: whether perfect love really demands visible presence, and whether we can know that the seekers are truly nonresistant. Both are still debated. This node does not decide — it only makes sure the question is asked honestly.
