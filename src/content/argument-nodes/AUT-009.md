# AUT-009 -- The Strongest Objection: The Claim That Cannot Be Tested

## The claim in plain words

Here is the objection that must be spoken clearly: the church's claim of authority cannot be tested. Scripture? Its interpretation is determined by the magisterium. History? Its judgment is disputed. Fruits? The scale is held by the party being tested. Clifford demands: "It is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone, to believe anything upon insufficient evidence."

If every test borrows the claim itself, then the claim has not passed any test — it is accepted through choice. That does not prove the claim false. It proves that honesty demands calling it a choice, not evidence.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This node does not prove the magisterium mistaken, and does not prove that there is no reason to believe. It proves something smaller but more unsettling: there is no test free of borrowing — and the final decision is not the result of testing, but a choice in the presence of testing. That burden is carried to the next chapter.
