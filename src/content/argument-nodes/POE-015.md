# POE-015 — Catholic Providence and the Limits of Human Knowledge

## Plain-language statement

The Catholic tradition places the problem of evil within a larger theological frame. The Catechism (§§309–314) teaches that God freely creates a world in a state of journeying toward its ultimate perfection (§310); that moral evil entered the world through the free misuse of creaturely freedom and that God is in no way its cause (§311); that God is able to bring good from the consequences of evil, even moral evil, the cross and resurrection being the central example (§312); and that the full understanding of the ways of providence is not available to us in this life, but will be known only at the eschatological vision 'face to face' (§314).

The tradition does not claim to have a complete theodicy. It claims to have placed the problem within a frame that is honest about what is known and what is not. The chapter respects the honesty and refuses to over-claim. The reframing is not a solution; it is a way of carrying the question.

## What this argument does NOT establish

This argument does not establish that the Catholic tradition has a full theodicy. It does not establish that any particular natural evil has a particular cause. It does not establish that the eschatological vision will vindicate the framing. It only establishes that the Catholic tradition has a way of holding the problem of evil without dissolving it, and that the holding is honest about the present-life epistemic limits.
